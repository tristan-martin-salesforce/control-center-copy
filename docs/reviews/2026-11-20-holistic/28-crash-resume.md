# 28 · Crash and resume: the worker killed mid-scan (post-crash-resume, 2026-11-21)

Ask #14 ("out-of-the-box for non-technical users with end-to-end error handling"): a teammate's Mac sleeps, loses Wi-Fi, runs out of disk, or the worker is killed mid-scan, and nothing is lost, duplicated or silently stuck; what happened is visible in Health in plain words. The rules this lane settled are in [EDGE-CASES.md "Worker"](../../EDGE-CASES.md#worker-crashes-sleep-and-outages-lane-post-crash-resume-ask-14) (W-rules 1-9, rows WK-01..WK-15).

## How it was drilled

`tests/db/worker/crash-resume.test.ts` (db project, its own database `control_center_crashdrill_<suffix>`). For each fault point a real worker process (`tests/db/worker/crash-child.ts`: the production `buildScanning` + `WorkerRuntime` + `DbScannerStore` over Postgres) runs a three-page Gmail-like scan (`tests/db/worker/crash-drill.ts`: six placeholder messages, two per page, continuations between pages) with a fake classifier that behaves like the FTI runner (one `pass` trace row per paid call, `seen_decided` from stored verdicts, verdicts saved last, one ask per message, one link to a standing work item on page 1). The child SIGKILLs itself at the fault point; no flush, no drain. A new worker then starts on the same database the way `src/worker/index.ts` starts (recovery, `dropStalePlans`, reap, plan, claim) and drains. Every point asserts the same invariants:

- no scan run and no job is `running`; the interrupted run is `cancelled`;
- six source items and six asks, each exactly once; the cursor at now and no coverage hole over the window;
- before the restart: every message behind the cursor is stored and has its ask (the watermark never passed unwritten data);
- the standing work item the scan linked to has a re-evaluation job;
- every `pass` row has exactly one `llm_calls` row (`pass:<run>:<seq>`), no hash twice, each at the call's price;
- no spend hold left `open`;
- Health: one `WORKER.SCAN_INTERRUPTED` row, closed with "Inbox: a scan stopped when the app closed; it picked up where it left off."

The baseline (before any fix) was taken with the same drill and a no-op recovery, once as the worker started then and once with the 90 s leases forced to expire (what the reaper would do a minute and a half later).

## Follow-up: job kinds the gate and recovery know (post-job-kinds, 24a41aa)

The worker-follow-ups lane noted that `LLM_JOB_KINDS` named `readin.research` and `vdp.assets` where the real kinds are `readin.generate` and `vdp.assets.find`: the start gate never held for those jobs, and `releaseDeadHolds` (which only knows listed kinds) never released a dead read-in's or Find assets' open hold at start (only the 6 h sweep did). **Resolved:** the table names the real kinds and every other handler that can call a model (`spends: true` on its registry entry, `tests/jobs/job-kinds.test.ts`); the gate's hold is the one those two jobs run under (`tests/db/worker/job-kinds-spend.spec.ts`).

## Follow-up: one hold per relay job (post-relay-holds, b0b4ad5)

The job-kinds lane left one note: `vdp.specialist_aes`, `accounts.hierarchy`, `org62.readback` and `org62.discover` got the gate's hold plus a hold per lookup (`relayGuard`), so the day was not charged twice but was held twice while the job ran, and the hold records reported the spend twice, which skewed the overrun median. **Resolved:** the gate's hold is the job's one hold. `relayGuard` finds it (`startJobSpend` with `existingOnly`: the gate's by `<kind>:<id>`, or the one a continuation carries), books each lookup's cost into it and opens none of its own; between lookups a passed hold raises one SPEND.ASK, and the job keeps what it read and queues its continuation on the same hold (`vars.relayDone`: the read-back reads on from the batch it stopped at). A resumed hold settles in the guard, the gate's in the worker, and crash recovery releases them as before (the kinds are listed). With no job hold (a direct call, no gate) the per-lookup hold stays as the fallback. Drill: `tests/db/worker/relay-holds.spec.ts` (four kinds); unit: `tests/jobs/relay-guard.test.ts`.

## Flake: relay holds under load (post-relay-flake)

**Seen:** in one full `CC_TEST_DB_SUFFIX=integ20 npm run test:db` (two Next builds running), both `org62.readback` cases of `tests/db/worker/relay-holds.spec.ts` failed once and passed on every rerun.

**Cause: not a clock, a shared queue.** The 12 `db-worker` files share one database, and `claimJobs` (`src/db/queue.ts`) takes any due job on a pool's queues, oldest `run_at` first, with no owner filter (one user per install). `schedules.spec.ts` leaves a due `doctor.checks` job (another user's) queued on `maintenance`, the read-back's queue. Vitest orders files by their cached duration and last failure, and a loaded machine changes those durations. When `schedules.spec.ts` ran first, the read-back test's worker (concurrency 1) claimed `doctor.checks` (no handler: `unknown_kind`) in place of its own job. `idle()` returned with 0 lookups (`expected [] to have a length of 2`). The read-back job then ran late, from the worker's post-job `setImmediate` poll, and the Stop case saw 4 lookups. The other three kinds' queues (`vdp-assets`, `vdp`, `attribution`) had no due leftover, so only the read-back failed.

**Reproduced:** a stranger's due job enqueued on each case's real queue in `beforeAll` failed 5 of the 8 cases (both read-back cases with the same two symptoms). It passed after the fix.

**Fix (the test's assumption, not the code):** each case's handler runs on a queue of its own (`<queue>.relayholds.<uuid>`; the continuation follows `ctx.job.queue`). The stranger's due jobs stay in `beforeAll` as the regression guard, a last case asserts they are still queued, and `afterAll` cancels them so no later file finds them due. `tests/db/worker/job-kinds-spend.spec.ts` (same pattern on `readin` and `vdp`) also gets a per-file queue, because the stranger's `vdp` job made it fail the same way in one run before `afterAll` cancelled it. No timeout was changed.

**Ruled out:**
- The `today` boundary: the test user, `recordSpend`'s `budgetDay` and the governor's `today()` all resolve America/Denver (`createUser`, settings default), and the hard cap of $15 is never close.
- Continuation `run_at`: `SPEND_ASK_RECHECK_S` is 6 h, so it is never claimable before Allow.
- Lease and job timeouts (90 s and 900 s against a test of about 200 ms).
- Hold ordering by `created_at` (one hold per user).
- History-based estimates (`costSamples` is per user).

5 runs of the file under 12 `yes` hogs passed (tests took 2.3 s against 0.2 s); load alone does not trip it.

**Resolved (ded02af, post-claim-owner):** was open: a `WorkerRuntime` runs any claimed job with its own `userId` (`ctx.userId = this.o.userId`), whoever owns the job. On a one-user install that is the same user. If one database ever serves two users' workers, `claimJobs` needs an owner filter (or the worker should run as `job.ownerUserId`).

`claimJobs` takes `ownerUserId` and the `WorkerRuntime` passes its own `userId`, so a worker claims only its user's jobs (option (a); still an index scan on `jobs_dequeue_idx`). `relay-holds.spec.ts` runs on the real queues again, with the stranger's due jobs there as the guard (7 of 9 cases fail without the filter); `worker.spec.ts` "claims only its own user's jobs" covers two users on one queue; `job-kinds-spend.spec.ts` keeps its per-file queue.
